**To:** Hospital Executive Committee
**From:** Safaratu Mala Binga, Healthcare Data Analyst
**Re:** Patient outcomes review, 20 facilities, Jan 2022 to Oct 2024 (200 admissions)

---

**Bottom line.** Outcomes across the network are poor on every headline measure, but the data behind them is not yet reliable enough to target individual wards, hospitals or doctors. I recommend fixing three data gaps immediately and, in parallel, launching two low-risk programmes that work whatever the final numbers turn out to be.

**What the data shows**

| Measure | Network | Typical benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| 30-day readmission | **54.5%** | 10–20% |
| In-hospital mortality | **22.5%** | well under 10% for general admissions |
| Left against medical advice | **24.5%** | low single digits |
| Bill collected | **66.2%** (₦28.8m of ₦85.3m outstanding) | — |
| Average stay | 23.2 days | — |

* **Readmissions** are high across every diagnosis, insurance type and age group. They are highest for malaria (67%), NHIS patients (65%) and young adults (71%). No group or doctor is statistically different from the rest, which points to a **system-wide discharge problem**, not a few bad actors.
* **Mortality** is highest in Paediatrics (35%, 14 of 40) and at HOSP004 (41%, 7 of 17).
* **One in four patients leaves against advice**, rising to one in three for weekend admissions.
* **Billing does not track care**: on average a 40-day stay is billed about the same as a 4-day one, and an ICU bed-day is only 15% dearer than a general bed. Self-paying patients appear to go home about 6 days earlier than insured patients.

**Why caution is needed.** The extract has 200 admissions, not the 3,000 expected, and no satisfaction scores. Also, 34 of the 40 "Paediatric" patients are adults and half of "Maternity" patients are male, so ward labels are wrong for many records. Rates at the level of a single hospital or doctor rest on 5–18 patients.

**Recommendations**

1. **Fix the data (owner: Medical Records/IT, 30 days).** Correct ward coding at admission. Add a 1–5 satisfaction question at discharge. Agree one definition of readmission. Re-run this analysis on the full dataset. *Alongside this:* clinical leads review all 14 Paediatric deaths and the 7 deaths at HOSP004.
2. **Discharge and follow-up programme (owner: Nursing/Pharmacy, pilot in 2 hospitals for 90 days).** Before discharge, confirm the patient has the full medication course. Call at day 7 and book a clinic visit, starting with NHIS, malaria, typhoid and diabetes patients. **Target: halve readmissions in pilot sites.** Review doctor-level readmissions monthly on a funnel chart (DR011 and DR044 are nearest the alert line).
3. **Keep patients and revenue (owner: Operations/Finance).** Put a senior clinician on weekend cover. Give patients a cost estimate and discharge counselling at admission to reduce leaving against advice. Introduce HMO/NHIS pre-authorisation and 30-day claims follow-up to lift collection from 66% towards 80%.

**Decision requested:** approve the 30-day data fix and the 90-day pilot. I will report pilot results and full-dataset findings at the next committee meeting.
